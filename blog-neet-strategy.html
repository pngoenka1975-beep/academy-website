NEET 700+ Strategy: Complete Preparation Guide

By Rising Starz Mentors • 12 min read • Updated January 2026

12 min read Jan 15, 2026 Expert Verified

Scoring 700+ in NEET UG is a dream for thousands of medical aspirants. With the right strategy, consistent NCERT-based preparation, and expert guidance, it's absolutely achievable. In this guide, Rising Starz's senior mentors break down the exact roadmap to crack NEET with a top score.

1. Understand the NEET Exam Pattern

Before diving into preparation, you must know what you're up against. NEET UG consists of 180 questions (45 each from Physics, Chemistry, Botany, and Zoology) with a total of 720 marks. Each correct answer gives +4 marks, and each incorrect answer deducts 1 mark.

  • Total Marks: 720
  • Total Questions: 180
  • Duration: 3 hours 20 minutes
  • Negative Marking: -1 for wrong answer
  • Target for 700+: Approximately 165–170 correct answers

2. NCERT is Your Bible

NEET is fundamentally an NCERT-based exam. Approximately 85–90% of questions are directly or indirectly derived from NCERT textbooks (Class 11 and 12). Your first priority must be completing NCERT line-by-line.

  • Read every line of NCERT Biology (Botany + Zoology) carefully.
  • Highlight diagrams, tables, and examples — NEET loves diagram-based questions.
  • For Chemistry, focus on NCERT exercises and in-text questions.
  • For Physics, understand the derivations and formulas from NCERT first, then move to advanced problems.

3. Subject-Wise Strategy

Biology (Botany & Zoology) — 360 Marks

Biology carries the highest weightage and is the most scoring section for a 700+ target.

  • Complete NCERT Class 11 and 12 Biology thoroughly.
  • Practice previous year questions (PYQs) from the last 15 years.
  • Focus on high-weightage chapters: Genetics, Ecology, Human Physiology, Plant Physiology, and Cell Biology.
  • Draw and label diagrams repeatedly — they frequently appear in NEET.

Chemistry — 180 Marks

Divide your preparation into Physical, Organic, and Inorganic Chemistry.

  • Physical Chemistry: Focus on mole concept, thermodynamics, and equilibrium. Practice numericals daily.
  • Organic Chemistry: Master reaction mechanisms, named reactions, and NCERT examples.
  • Inorganic Chemistry: NCERT is the primary source — memorize periodic trends, p-block, d-block, and coordination compounds.

Physics — 180 Marks

Physics is often the toughest section for medical aspirants. Build concepts before solving problems.

  • Start with NCERT Physics for concept clarity.
  • High-weightage topics: Modern Physics, Semiconductors, Current Electricity, Thermodynamics, and Optics.
  • Maintain a formula notebook and revise it weekly.
  • Solve PYQs to understand the pattern of numerical questions.

4. Create a Realistic Study Timetable

Consistency beats intensity. A balanced daily routine ensures all three subjects get adequate attention.

  • Morning (7:30 AM – 11:30 AM): Toughest subject first (usually Physics or Chemistry)
  • Afternoon (2:00 PM – 5:00 PM): Biology — NCERT reading and diagram practice
  • Evening (6:00 PM – 8:00 PM): Practice questions and PYQs
  • Night (8:30 PM – 10:00 PM): Revision of the day's topics and doubt clearing

5. Practice with Previous Year Question Papers

Solving NEET PYQs from 2013–2025 helps you understand the question pattern, difficulty level, and frequently repeated topics. Aim to solve at least 10–15 full-length papers under timed conditions before the exam.

6. Mock Tests and Performance Analysis

Take weekly full-length mock tests. Analyze every mistake — whether it's a conceptual error, silly calculation mistake, or time management issue. Use AI-powered test analytics (available at Rising Starz) to pinpoint weak chapters.

7. Revision Strategy

  • Daily: 30 minutes revision of what you studied that day.
  • Weekly: Sunday revision of the entire week's topics.
  • Monthly: Full syllabus mock test and comprehensive revision.
  • Last 30 Days: Focus only on revision, PYQs, and mock tests — no new topics.

8. Health and Mindset

  • Sleep 7–8 hours daily — a tired brain cannot retain information.
  • Take short breaks every 90 minutes (Pomodoro technique).
  • Stay hydrated and eat nutritious meals.
  • Avoid social media distractions during study hours.
  • Maintain a positive mindset — believe in your preparation.
